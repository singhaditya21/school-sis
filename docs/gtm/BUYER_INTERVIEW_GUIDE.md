# Buyer Interview Guide

## Research objective

Determine whether multi-campus fee reconciliation and governance is sufficiently frequent, severe, budgeted, and poorly served to support a paid ScholarMind pilot.

Do not lead with a product tour. Ask about the last real occurrence, the current process, consequences, ownership, and procurement behavior before presenting a hypothesis.

## Required interview mix

- Five economic buyers: trustee, CEO, COO, or CFO.
- Five operational champions: group finance, accounts, or MIS lead.
- Three risk/technical gatekeepers: IT, security, ERP, or implementation lead.
- Two ecosystem experts: auditor, CA, payment provider, migration consultant, or former buyer.

## Opening

“We are studying how multi-campus school groups consolidate fee data, reconcile collections, approve sensitive exceptions, and report to central leadership. This is a research conversation, not a sales presentation. I would like to understand the most recent real process and where it breaks.”

Ask permission to take notes. Do not record without explicit consent.

## Core questions

### Context

1. How many campuses, students, boards, legal entities, and finance teams are involved?
2. Which systems manage students, fees, payments, accounting, and group reporting?
3. Which decisions are centralized and which are owned by campuses?

### Most recent workflow

4. Walk me through the most recent month-end fee reconciliation from source data to executive sign-off.
5. Which exports, spreadsheets, manual checks, and people were involved?
6. How long did it take, and where did the team wait or repeat work?
7. Which mismatches or exceptions were difficult to explain?

### Consequence and priority

8. What happens when collections, waivers, refunds, or settlements are wrong or late?
9. What is the approximate staff time, delayed cash, audit exposure, or management attention involved?
10. Among your top three operating priorities this year, where does this problem rank?
11. What event would cause leadership to act now rather than next year?

### Existing alternatives

12. What have you already tried—ERP modules, spreadsheets, BI, auditors, payment portals, or custom integrations?
13. What works well in the current system and must not be disrupted?
14. What would make a replacement or coexistence project too risky?

### Buying process

15. Who owns the budget, who champions the work, and who can block it?
16. What security, data, contracting, and procurement reviews are required?
17. What would a small paid pilot need to prove before a group rollout?
18. What price range would make the pilot feel trivial, reasonable, expensive, or impossible?

## Concept test after discovery

Only after completing the questions, present this neutral description:

“A control layer imports existing student, fee, and transaction data, reconciles it, surfaces exceptions, routes waiver/refund approvals, and produces group reporting without requiring the existing ERP to be replaced during the pilot.”

Ask:

- What is most useful?
- What is unnecessary?
- What would make this untrustworthy?
- What would need to be true for you to fund a pilot?
- What data could you realistically provide for a workshop?

## Evidence scoring

| Score | Evidence quality |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | Opinion or hypothetical interest only |
| 1 | General pain with no recent example |
| 2 | Recent example and current workaround |
| 3 | Quantified consequence and named owner |
| 4 | Active buying trigger, process, and budget range |
| 5 | Agrees to data workshop, diligence, or paid proposal |

An interview supports the wedge only when it scores at least 3 and the problem is ranked in the buyer’s top three.

## Required notes

Capture role, group size, systems, last incident, frequency, consequence, current workaround, priority rank, buyer/champion/gatekeeper, data availability, price response, exact next step, and evidence link. Do not store sensitive student or payment data in the research tracker.
